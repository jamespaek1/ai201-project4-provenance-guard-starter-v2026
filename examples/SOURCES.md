# Calibration specimen provenance

These seven small specimens were selected or written for the Unit 7 instructor
example on **2026-10-21**, before their detector outputs were seen. They are
**development examples, not the Unit 8 attack set, an independent test set, or
an estimate of detection accuracy**. Their actual provenance is known; no
particular classifier output is assumed correct merely because it is high or
low. Every complete text, whitespace word count and SHA-256 hash is stored in
[calibration.json](calibration.json).

## Human excerpts

The three human texts predate modern language models. They are quoted excerpts,
not paraphrases generated by the assistant. None was written by the user, and
no human review or personal writing exercise is claimed.

- **Lewis Carroll, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865).** The opening two
  paragraphs of Chapter I, from “Alice was beginning” through “ran close by
  her.” The [Project Gutenberg transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11/pg11-images.html)
  supplies the exact prose. Its [catalog record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11)
  identifies the author and confirms public-domain status in the USA. Paragraph
  separation is normalized to two newline characters; wording and displayed
  punctuation are preserved.
- **Declaration of Independence (1776).** The complete paragraph beginning
  “We hold these truths” and ending “Safety and Happiness.” The
  [National Archives transcription](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript)
  is the source. It states that it transcribes the Stone Engraving and preserves
  the original spelling and punctuation. The specimen retains its capitalization,
  double hyphens and spacing. This is a public-domain historical document, not
  the surrounding modern website commentary.
- **James Joyce, *Ulysses* (1922).** Part I, section [3], opening text from
  “Ineluctable modality” through “Shut your eyes and see.” The
  [Project Gutenberg transcription](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4300/pg4300-images.html)
  supplies the exact prose. The [catalog record](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4300)
  identifies the pre-1923 edition and confirms public-domain status in the USA.
  Paragraph separation is normalized to two newline characters; displayed
  wording and punctuation are preserved. The short Italian phrase is part of
  the original predominantly English passage.

Source pages were checked on the date above. The literary excerpts are from
public-domain works; Project Gutenberg's [reuse terms](https://www.gutenberg.org/policy/license.html)
remain available with its editions. Famous passages may already be familiar to
GPT-2 through its training data. That is an additional reason these fixtures
cannot establish real-world detection quality.

## AI-authored and mixed specimens

`ai_generic` was composed by Codex AI as an ordinary explanation of a community
garden. It describes a fictional general situation and does not claim that a
real garden or volunteer event occurred.

`ai_repetitive` was composed by Codex AI using twelve parallel sentences about a
team's project plan, task list and meeting date. It deliberately tests a regular
writing pattern. It is an extreme construction, not a representative sample of
AI writing, and was not selected in response to a detector score.

`ai_lightly_edited` preserves most of `ai_generic`, with three disclosed edit
operations: split one sentence and add “too”; replace a planning sentence with
a question and follow-up; introduce the typo “comunication” and replace a phrase
with “keep things moving.” **The editor was Codex AI.** It is not evidence of
human editing, and the modified specimen remains AI-authored.

`mixed_narrative` uses the unchanged first paragraph of the Carroll excerpt,
followed by a new paragraph beginning “Alice decided” that Codex AI wrote. The
blank line marks the authorship boundary. This is known mixed authorship, so
neither a pure-human nor pure-AI reference label is claimed for the whole text.

These fixtures are committed for reproducibility. Future accuracy measurement
should use an independently selected corpus, with provenance checked before
scoring and no overlap with these development examples.
